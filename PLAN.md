# 12-Week Recomposition & Calisthenics Plan

**Start:** 177 cm · 64 kg · ~27% BF · 8 push-ups · 0 pull-ups · 10s hollow · 0s handstand
**Target:** ~20% BF · first pull-up · 3-minute plank · 30s wall handstand → freestanding kick-up

---

## 0. Read this first — the math on your goal

Your numbers mean this is a **recomposition**, not a weight-loss problem.

| | Now | Target as stated | What actually gets you there |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weight | 64 kg | 60 kg | 61–62 kg |
| Fat mass | 17.3 kg | 12.0 kg | 12.2 kg |
| Lean mass | 46.7 kg | 48.0 kg | 49–50 kg |

To go from 27% to 20% you must **lose ~5 kg of fat while gaining ~2 kg of muscle**. At 177 cm and 64 kg you are not carrying excess weight — you are carrying too little muscle. If you crash-diet to 60 kg you will arrive at roughly 24% body fat and look softer than you do now.

**So: stop targeting 60 kg.** Target the waist tape and the mirror. If you build the muscle properly you may sit at 62 kg and look dramatically leaner than 60 kg would have.

Two more things:
- Your Eufy scale's body-fat reading is bioimpedance. It is accurate to about ±5% and swings with hydration. Use it for **trend only**, never as a single number.
- Realistic timeline for 5 kg of fat at a muscle-sparing rate: **16–20 weeks.** This document covers 12. You will be most of the way there.

## 1. The weekly structure

Your constraint is not motivation, it is 12 hours a day at a desk. That is the single biggest thing working against you — it costs you hip mobility, thoracic extension, shoulder position, and recovery. The daily 30-minute block exists to fight it. Treat it as non-negotiable infrastructure, not optional extra.

| Day | Session | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Mon** | Strength A — Push | 60 min |
| **Tue** | Daily block: Mobility B (shoulders/wrists) + handstand | 30 min |
| **Wed** | Strength B — Pull | 60 min |
| **Thu** | Swim | 30–40 min |
| **Fri** | Strength C — Legs & Core | 60 min |
| **Sat** | Hyrox | 60–75 min |
| **Sun** | Daily block: Mobility A + walk. Thai massage here. | 30 min |

On strength days (Mon/Wed/Fri) the 30-minute daily block is **mobility only** — no extra loading. Do it in the evening, away from the session. On Tue/Thu/Sun the block *is* the session.

**Deload:** Week 6 — cut every set count in half, keep the loads. **Test weeks:** end of Week 4, 8, 12.

## 3. Phase 1 — Weeks 1–4: Foundation

Goal: own the movement patterns, build the tendon and connective-tissue base, get a negative pull-up.

Every session opens with the same 6-minute prep:

**Universal warm-up**
1. Wrist circles + wrist rocks (palms down, palms up) — 60s
2. Shoulder CARs — 5 slow circles each arm
3. Cat-cow — 10
4. 90/90 hip switches — 10 each side
5. Glute bridge — 15
6. Scapular push-up — 10

### Session A — Push (Mon)

| # | Exercise | Sets × Reps | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Incline push-up | 4 × 8–12 | Hands on bench. Lower the bench each week as reps come easily. |
| 2 | DB overhead press (seated) | 3 × 8–10 | Ribs down, don't arch. |
| 3 | Goblet squat | 3 × 10 | Heels flat, knees track over toes. |

## 4. Phase 2 — Weeks 5–8: Build

Same three sessions, these changes:

- **Incline push-up → floor push-up.** 4 × 6–10. When you hit 4×10, move to feet-elevated.
- **Negative pull-up → band-assisted pull-up.** 4 × 4. Reduce band thickness as you progress.
- **Add DB bench press** to Session A: 3 × 8.
- **Add weight** to Bulgarian split squats and goblet squats. Add 2.5 kg when you hit the top of the rep range on all sets.
- **Wall walk → chest-to-wall handstand hold.** 4 × 20–40s.
- **Hollow hold → 4 × 30s legs straight.**
- **Plank: 3 × 60s.**
- Add **Copenhagen plank** 2 × 15s each side to Session C.

## 5. Phase 3 — Weeks 9–12: Consolidate

- **Pull-up attempts** at the start of Session B, before anything else, 5 singles with 90s rest. Then band-assisted work.
- **Feet-elevated push-ups** 4 × 8–12. If easy, add a slow 3s lowering phase.
- **Freestanding handstand kick-ups** — 10 attempts against a wall, aiming to find balance for 1–2 seconds without touching.
- **Weighted carries and split squats** progress every week.
- **Plank: 2 × 90s.** Test the 3-minute in Week 12.

### Swim (Thu)
30–40 min. Freestyle and backstroke. Backstroke in particular opens the front of your shoulders and chest. Not a hard session — this is active recovery and posture work. 4 × 100 m easy, 4 × 50 m moderate, 200 m cool down, adjust to your level.

15 min of Mobility A or B, evening, plus a 15-minute walk. Nothing loaded.

### Desk protocol — every day, costs nothing
Once an hour: stand, 30s couch stretch each side, 10 shoulder rolls, 5 scapular squeezes. Set a timer. This matters more than any single stretching session.

## 7. Hyrox day (Sat)

Hyrox is high-fatigue and you are a beginner. In Phase 1, go at **conversational effort**, focus on movement quality, and scale everything. Do not race it. It is your conditioning, not a second strength session.

**Beginner-scaled template (60 min):**
- 800 m run @ easy pace
- 15 wall balls (4 kg) — squat depth is the point, not speed
- 400 m run
- 15 sandbag lunges (light) or bodyweight walking lunges
- 400 m run
- 20 burpee step-outs (no jump)
- 400 m run
- 40 m farmer carry
- 400 m run
- 500 m row or SkiErg
- Cool down 10 min

Progress by adding a round or reducing rest, not by adding load, until Week 8.

**If Saturday leaves you wrecked into Monday, cut it to every other week.** Recovery is the limiting factor with a 12-hour desk day, not effort.

## 8. Nutrition

Your maintenance is roughly **2,100 kcal**. You want a small deficit — large enough to lose fat, small enough to build muscle. Aggressive dieting at your body composition would be self-defeating.

**Daily targets**

| | Amount |
|---|---|
| Calories | 1,900–2,000 |
| **Protein** | **130 g** — the non-negotiable number |
| Fat | 60–70 g |
| Carbs | 190–210 g |

Expected rate: **0.25–0.4 kg/week.** Anything faster and you are losing muscle, which is the opposite of the goal.

**Practical protein** — 130 g is the hard part. Rough anchors: 100 g chicken breast ≈ 31 g · 3 eggs ≈ 18 g · 170 g Greek yoghurt ≈ 17 g · 1 scoop whey ≈ 25 g · 100 g salmon ≈ 25 g · 150 g cottage cheese ≈ 17 g · 100 g lentils cooked ≈ 9 g. Hitting it usually means protein at every meal plus one shake, not one big protein dinner.

**Around training:** eat carbs in the meal before your strength sessions. Protein within a couple of hours after. Nothing more complicated than that is needed.

**Rest days:** drop 20–30 g of carbs if you want; optional, not important.

**Hydration:** 2.5–3 L, more on swim and Hyrox days.

**Alcohol:** the single easiest lever. It blunts protein synthesis and adds calories with no satiety. Not zero, but few and infrequent.

## 10. Sleep

7 hours is the floor, not the target. With 12-hour work days and 5 training days, push for **7.5–8 on Sunday, Tuesday and Thursday** at minimum. Muscle is built in sleep, and your handstand progress in particular is a neurological skill — it consolidates overnight. If you can only fix one input outside the gym, fix this one.

## 11. Thai massage, TCM and herbs

Keep the Thai massage. Weekly soft-tissue work is genuinely useful for someone sitting 12 hours a day, and Sunday is the right slot for it.

On Chinese medicine and herbs, honestly: acupuncture and massage are low-risk and many people find them helpful for tension and sleep. Herbal formulas are a different category and worth a little care.

- Buy only from a licensed practitioner or an established supplier. Adulteration and heavy-metal contamination in unregulated herbal products is a documented and real problem.
- Some formulas affect liver enzymes or interact with medications. Tell your practitioner everything you take, including supplements.
- Do not expect herbs to move body composition. Nothing in that category will meaningfully change your body fat. Judge them on how you sleep and feel, not on the scale.

Treat it as recovery and stress management alongside the plan, not as a lever on the plan.

## 13. The four rules that matter most

1. **Protein at 130 g, every day.** Everything else in the nutrition section is negotiable. This is not.
2. **Progress the log, not the feeling.** Add a rep, a second, or 2.5 kg. Write it down. A session that felt hard but matched last week is not progress.
3. **Recovery is your bottleneck, not effort.** With 12-hour desk days, the failure mode is doing too much, not too little. If sleep drops or sessions get worse for two weeks, cut volume by a third.
4. **Stop weighing the goal in kilograms.** Waist, photos, and the pull-up bar tell you the truth. The scale does not.
